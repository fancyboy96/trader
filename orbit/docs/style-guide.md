## Voice

Direct and factual. State what is true; let the data carry the weight. Avoid rhetorical framing that editorialises beyond what the numbers show.

---

## Punctuation

**No em-dashes in prose.**

Em-dashes (`—`) interrupt flow and are often used as a crutch to insert asides or dramatic pivots. Replace them:

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| `contracts — fewer than 0.3% — account for` | `contracts (fewer than 0.3%) account for` |
| `Defence — 57 cents in every dollar` | `Defence, or 57 cents in every dollar,` |
| `operators — the infrastructure of operations —` | `operators, the infrastructure of operations,` |

En-dashes (`–`) in date ranges and number spans are fine: `FY2024–FY2025`, `$1M–$5M`.

---

## Contrast framing

**Do not use "not X" or "rather than X" as the rhetorical payoff of a sentence.**

Contrast framing positions the thing being dismissed as the expected or implied answer, then corrects it. This is editorialising. State the positive fact directly.

**Avoid:**
- `signals systems integration scale, not advisory day-rates`
- `reflects timing, not trajectory`
- `a structural barrier, not merely a commercial one`
- `The Australian Commonwealth is not a meaningful MBB market`
- `indicating a large platform award in the current year rather than gradual contract accumulation`

**Rewrite as the affirmative fact:**
- `reflects systems integration scale`
- `reflects award timing`
- `a structural barrier`
- `Combined MBB spend over three years is approximately $40M`
- `indicating a large platform award concentrated in the current year`

---

## Sentence construction

- Lead with the subject and the fact. Subordinate clauses follow.
- One idea per sentence where the idea is significant.
- Avoid stacking qualifications before the claim: `"While it is true that... one might argue... it could be said that..."` — cut all of it.
- Short sentences are fine. They do not need to be padded.

---

## Numbers and statistics

- Anchor percentages to their denominator on first reference: `62% of all value` not `62%`.
- Use parentheses to inline clarifying figures: `Home Affairs ($10.8B), Health ($6.6B)`.
- Avoid writing the same figure in two forms in the same sentence: `$112.5B, or 62% of all spend` is enough — do not add `the vast majority` or similar.

---

## Things to avoid

- Superlatives used as filler: `highly`, `significantly`, `dramatically`
- Announcing the insight before stating it: `The structurally significant finding is...`
- Hedge stacking: `may potentially suggest`
- Rhetorical questions
- Summary sentences that restate the preceding paragraph
