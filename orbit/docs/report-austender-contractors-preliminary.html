Orbit — Preliminary Analysis

The Government Contractor Industry: Market Structure and Supplier Profiles

Source: AusTender OCDS API  ·  Contractors: 33,086 (excl. pass-through accounts)  ·  Total spend analysed: $203.7B  ·  Generated: 14 April 2026
Overview
The Commonwealth procurement base contains 33,086 contractors, functioning as two distinct industries operating in parallel. The first is a concentrated tier of roughly 1,500 established suppliers (primes, estate managers, IT integrators, consultants, labour hire firms, and specialist monopolies) holding long-term, multi-year relationships worth $123B collectively. The second is a broad, shallow pool of 19,000+ one-time or single-year contractors: tradespeople, medical practitioners, regional businesses, and project-specific suppliers who collectively account for under 15% of total value.

The top 100 contractors hold 55% of all spend from a base of 33,000. The top 500 hold 80%. The 298 contractors with more than 100 contract awards between them hold 34% of value. A single government buyer transacts repeatedly with a small set of deeply embedded incumbents.

The most valuable contractor relationships are service monopolies. Austal ($6.9B, 4 contracts) and Hanwha ($5.2B, 5 contracts) are the only viable domestic shipbuilding and armoured vehicle suppliers. Secure Journeys ($4.7B, 2 contracts) holds Australia's immigration detention infrastructure. DHA ($4.4B) provides defence housing. Viva Energy ($2.0B) supplies fuel to Defence estates. In each case the contract value reflects the absence of a competitive alternative.

A structurally significant segment sits outside the conventional contractor picture: property trusts and single-purpose entities receiving billion-dollar payments for office leases classified as procurement. The Trustee for 150 Lonsdale Street Trust ($1.05B, one contract, Australian Federal Police), Athllon Trustee ($368M, Services Australia), and a cluster of ATO property vehicles reveal that a material portion of AusTender's recorded "contractor" spend represents long-term lease payments.

Supplier Archetypes

Six distinct supplier types account for the structured portion of government spend. Archetype is inferred from contractor name and contract patterns using keyword matching; UNSPSC-based formal classification has not yet been applied.

Labour Hire & Recruitment
$3.7B
~198 firms
Contingent workforce supply across every agency. Hays alone spans 97 agencies. Contracts are small individually but numerous; volume is the business model.

Examples: Hays ($659M, 97 agencies), Hudson ($307M), Randstad ($239M), Adecco ($1.3B), HorizonOne ($112M)
Specialist Monopolies
~$15B+
Small number of sole-source providers
Single-source providers for critical services where government has no substitutable supplier. Contract values reflect structural dependency and sole-source status.

Examples: Secure Journeys ($4.7B, detention), DHA ($4.4B, defence housing), Viva Energy ($2.0B, fuel), Service Stream ($2.0B, one contract)
The six archetypes are structurally different markets. Defence primes and specialist monopolies operate with limited competitive tension; value reflects capability scarcity or sole-source status. Facilities and IT are competitive at the contract level but exhibit strong incumbent advantage at renewal. Consulting and labour hire are formally competitive but show persistent concentration: the same firms appear across dozens of agencies year after year.

Analytical Limitations
Contractor name fragmentation
The same company appears under multiple name variants across agency submissions. Segment totals and archetype groupings in this report aggregate known variants by keyword matching. Group-level spend for large firms (Deloitte, KPMG, Accenture) is understated where trading entity names were not identified. ABN-based consolidation, planned in Phase 1 of the enrichment pipeline, will resolve this.
Contract value is face value
AusTender records awarded contract value. Actual expenditure may differ due to contract variations, partial drawdown, or cancellation. Single-contract high-value records (Service Stream $2.0B, Sigma Healthcare $635M) should be treated as ceiling values pending confirmation against actual payments data.
Pass-through accounts excluded
The FMS Account (Reserve Bank of Australia, $23.3B) is excluded from all analysis. It is a financial channel for US Foreign Military Sales government-to-government payments. Similar cooperative program accounts (ACEA, DFAS-PE) are also excluded.
Archetype classification is heuristic
Supplier archetypes are inferred from contractor name keywords and contract patterns using heuristic matching; UNSPSC codes present in the dataset have not yet been applied. The "Other" category ($121.6B, 31,367 contractors) contains the majority of spend and includes many contractors that would classify into recognisable segments with richer data.
Methodology

Data is sourced from the AusTender OCDS API (api.tenders.gov.au/ocds), harvested via scripts/austender_harvest.py and stored in a local SQLite database (data/orbit.db). Pass-through financial accounts (FMS Account Reserve Bank of Australia and related cooperative programme accounts) are excluded from all contractor counts and spend totals in this report. Adjusted total spend is $203.7B across 33,086 contractors.

Concentration analysis: Contractors ranked by total awarded contract value. Top-N cumulative share calculated from ranked totals.

Tenure analysis: Years active calculated as distinct calendar years with at least one contract award. The dataset spans calendar years 2022–2026 (5 years).

Archetype classification: Name-keyword matching against known firm lists. Mutually exclusive: a contractor matching multiple keyword sets is assigned to the first matching category in priority order (Defence, Facilities, IT, Consulting, Labour Hire). The large "Other" residual reflects the limits of keyword-only classification.
