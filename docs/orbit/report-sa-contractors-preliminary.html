Orbit — Preliminary Analysis

South Australian Government Procurement — Contractor Analysis

Source: SA Government CKAN Open Data Portal  ·  Period: FY2024–FY2025 (dated records); prior years (undated records)  ·  Scope: 20 SA Government agencies  ·  Generated: 9 April 2026
Introduction
South Australian Government procurement disclosed in this dataset totals $3.57B across 2,370 contract records from 20 agencies. The structure of that spend is radically concentrated: the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) accounts for $3.24B — 91% of all disclosed value — across just 634 contracts. The remaining 19 agencies collectively represent the other 9%.

The dominant activity is capital infrastructure delivery. DIT is running the largest hospital building program in South Australia's history concurrently with major transport works — the Tram Grade Separation, Majors Road Interchange, and regional road upgrades. The top nine contracts by value are all construction awards over $50M. Within this, a small cohort of SA-based and national builders capture the bulk of spend: Built Environs, Built Pty Ltd, Sarah Constructions, McMahon Services, and Mossop Group between them hold over $1.29B.

Beyond DIT, the picture shifts entirely. SA Police spends almost exclusively on IT and administrative labour hire — Randstad, Hays, Paxus, Dialog, and Akkodis dominate a $143M total. SA Health's disclosed spend focuses on clinical reviews, workforce consulting, and pathology reform, with EY ($7.2M, 16 contracts) as its most embedded advisory firm and Scyne Advisory ($2.6M) handling internal audit. The Department for Education is a heavy labour hire consumer ($22M in one contract) alongside digital transformation professional services.

Professional services and consulting total $257M (7.2% of spend) when measured by known firm names. EY is present across 9 agencies and 31 contracts — the most broadly embedded advisory firm in the dataset. KPMG holds 35 contracts across 7 agencies. Scyne Advisory, active across Health, Education, and DIT, has established a meaningful post-PwC position in the SA government market. Labour hire firms — Hays, Randstad, Paxus, Akkodis — account for the largest consulting-adjacent spend, reflecting agencies' heavy reliance on contingent workforce sourcing.

The top 25 is almost entirely construction. Positions 1–21 are infrastructure builders or civil contractors, reflecting DIT's dominance of total disclosed spend. The only non-construction names entering the top 25 are Randstad (rank 22), Hays (rank 24), and Mark Davis (rank 25) — all engaged by SA Police and Education on labour hire.

Built Environs leads with $453.8M from just two contracts: the Flinders Medical Centre Redevelopment ($355.9M) and the Modbury Hospital Mental Health Precinct ($97.9M), both as managing contractor. Built Pty Ltd (a related but separate entity) holds a further $324.6M including Mt Barker Hospital ($289.8M in FY2025). Together, the Built group represents approximately $778M — roughly 22% of total disclosed spend — concentrated in hospital infrastructure.

The McConnell Dowell/CPB/Arup/Mott MacDonald JV ($404.6M) is a design-and-construct joint venture for the Tram Grade Separation Projects. This single contract — the largest by value in the dataset — involves all four firms acting together; individual shares are not disclosed.

McMahon Services ($165M, 16 contracts) and Mossop Group ($163M, 12 contracts) are the most active mid-tier builders by both value and contract count. McMahon spans hospitals, road safety, and regional infrastructure. Mossop is focused on mental health and hospital facility works across multiple DIT projects.

FY2025 shows a pipeline shift. Built Pty Ltd, BMD Constructions, Sitzler, Bild Infrastructure, CATCON, and Fulton Hogan all entered or expanded significantly in FY2025, while the FY2024 cohort (Built Environs, McConnell Dowell JV, Sarah Constructions, Acciona) was dominated by earlier awards. This suggests the capital program is progressing — some major FY2024 contracts are delivering while new packages are being awarded downstream.
Agency Analysis
Department for Infrastructure and Transport
634 contracts  ·  327 contractors
$3,240.1M
90.8% of total disclosed spend
DIT is running the most significant concurrent capital program in South Australia's recent history — hospital redevelopments, transport infrastructure, and regional road upgrades delivered simultaneously. The spend profile reflects this: the ten largest contracts are all over $30M, and the top three alone total $1.18B. The department's role as the state's capital delivery vehicle means it acts as principal for projects commissioned by other agencies (e.g., SA Health's hospital builds are procured and managed through DIT).

The dominant delivery model is the managing contractor — a construction management approach where the builder manages trade packages rather than undertaking a fixed-price lump sum. This model allocates risk differently from design-and-construct and tends to produce higher headline contract values. Built Environs, Built Pty Ltd, and Mossop Group are DIT's preferred managing contractors across the hospital program.

Beyond construction, DIT procures specialist engineering and architectural consulting for project initiation, design, and financial assurance. Baukultur ($17.3M) is the lead architect on the SA Forensic Centre. Swanbury Penglase ($8.5M), Grieve Gillett ($7.7M), and DesignInc Adelaide ($5.8M) are active on multiple DIT projects. Aurecon ($7.8M) and WSP ($5.7M) provide engineering advisory services. KPMG holds $6.5M across six contracts, predominantly for financial auditing of the North-South Corridor project alliance.

Soft facilities management for completed assets is held by Spotless Facility Services ($37.6M), indicating DIT's engagement extends into operational phase asset management post-construction.

South Australia Police
354 contracts  ·  95 contractors
$143.4M
4.0% of total disclosed spend
SAPOL's disclosed contractor spend is almost exclusively labour hire and contingent workforce. The agency publishes annual aggregate disclosures of agency staff costs categorised by function (IS&T, administrative, project support), rather than individual contract awards. The result is a spend profile dominated entirely by staffing firms, with individual contract values representing a full year of contingent workforce supply across each category.

IT and systems work drives the largest labour spend. Multiple SAPOL disclosures reference "IS&T activities" and "architecture and e-projects" — reflecting ongoing investments in police systems modernisation, records management, and digital infrastructure. Dialog ($9.5M, 5 contracts) stands out as SAPOL's primary IT services firm beyond pure recruitment, suggesting it operates in a more project-delivery capacity rather than simple labour supply. Escient ($5.4M, 5 contracts) similarly holds a position in IT advisory work.

Administrative and operational staffing runs concurrently — Randstad, Hays, and Paxus each hold multiple annual supply agreements covering general administrative, project, and COVID-era support functions. Akkodis ($6.2M) specialises in technology and engineering talent, consistent with SAPOL's IT-led spend profile.

The absence of significant professional services advisory — no strategy consulting, no Big 4 advisory, minimal management consulting — is notable. SAPOL's disclosed advisory footprint is contained: a single EY engagement ($56k) for an HR Management Branch Review is the only named advisory firm outside IT staffing.

SA Health
362 contracts  ·  ~120 contractors
$85.8M
2.4% of total disclosed spend
SA Health's disclosed spend combines three distinct categories: operational services (traffic control, pharmacy, clinical supply), advisory and reform consulting, and aggregate staffing disclosures from individual health networks. The largest single item is Workzone Traffic Control ($11.7M), reflecting the scale of on-site traffic management at COVID-19 drive-through testing sites operated across the network — a legacy of the pandemic response period.

The advisory and reform picture is more strategically significant. Ernst & Young is SA Health's most embedded advisory firm, with 16 disclosed engagements totalling $7.2M. EY's work spans three related bodies of work: the Safety and Efficiency Review of SA Pathology (multiple phases, total ~$3M), CALHN workforce modelling and planning ($1.86M across two contracts), and ongoing financial modelling, assurance, and data analytics support. EY was awarded the SA Pathology reform work competitively — a significant mandate and the largest individual advisory engagement in the health sector dataset.

Scyne Advisory holds $2.6M across 9 contracts covering internal audit, ways of working reform (organisational operating model, $1.46M), audit and financial advisory, contract management support, and cyber security review. Scyne's SA Health presence is consistent with its broader SA government positioning — a mid-market advisory firm with a strong audit and assurance base. FTI Consulting ($906k) was engaged for specific financial restructuring work.

A notable feature of SA Health's disclosures is the prevalence of pharmacy service contracts — RGH Pharmacy Consulting Services ($1.05M), Epic Pharmacy, and others — reflecting privatised or outsourced pharmacy operations at regional hospitals including Port Augusta and Whyalla. These are operational outsourcing arrangements, not advisory engagements.

Department for Education
349 contracts  ·  ~180 contractors
$64.0M
1.8% of total disclosed spend
Education's spend falls into two distinct streams: labour resources and digital transformation professional services. The largest single entry is a $22.2M "Labour resources" aggregated disclosure — by far the largest undifferentiated staffing contract in the non-DIT portion of the dataset. Schools Ministry Group ($5.7M) represents National Student Wellbeing Program delivery by a non-government provider.

The digital transformation and data program is the most analytically significant part of Education's spend. Multiple professional services contracts reference the Education Management System (EMS) project, an edAnalytics platform, ICT infrastructure in schools, and an Early Learning Strategy — reflecting a sustained investment in departmental data and systems capability. Expose Data ($1.9M, 4 contracts) is the primary analytics and data services firm across this program.

Labour hire (Hays $7.7M, Randstad $5.2M, Paxus $1.9M) reflects the department's use of contingent ICT and administrative workers across both operational and project roles. The Women's & Children's Hospital appears as a contractor ($1.4M) for Early Learning Strategy professional services — indicating cross-agency service delivery arrangements.

Scyne Advisory ($400k across 5 contracts) holds a foothold in Education covering EMS program assurance, procurement review, risk review, and program evaluation — a light-touch but broad advisory engagement consistent with Scyne's cross-government audit and assurance positioning.

Department of the Premier and Cabinet
120 contracts  ·  ~80 contractors
$10.0M
0.3% of total disclosed spend
DPC is the most advisory-intensive department relative to its size. 67% of its total spend ($6.7M of $10.0M) is professional services — a far higher advisory intensity than any other agency in the dataset. The department procures strategy, policy analysis, market research, ICT review, and programme evaluation rather than operational or capital goods.

The largest single engagement is Jacobs Group ($1.7M), contracted to conduct a constraints analysis for proposed battery storage sites for the SA Battery Storage Policy — a technically specialised infrastructure advisory assignment. Malcolm Hyde ($1.3M) was engaged for a review of multi-agency emergency response arrangements — a sensitive governance advisory mandate that would not have gone to open tender.

KPMG ($640k, 7 contracts) is DPC's most frequently used advisory firm, engaged across ICT functional reviews, assurance planning, and emerging issues. PwC ($375k, 6 contracts) holds a run of smaller advisory mandates. Square Holes ($574k) was engaged for marketing and communications strategy. Notable individual engagements include evaluation of the First Nations Entrepreneur Hub (Circle), economic impact analysis of Lot Fourteen, and several procurements listed as exempt from disclosure under PC027 — indicating sensitive or cabinet-related advisory work.

Elevate Management Consulting, Gus Commercial Consulting, and Innovation Performance Australia represent the boutique advisory tier — smaller SA-based firms holding project-specific mandates. The $350k Defence supply chain industry attraction engagement (awarded to Innovation Performance Australia) reflects DPC's role in economic policy coordination.

Labour hire dominates consulting-category spend. Randstad ($24.8M), Hays ($23.8M), Paxus ($15.9M), Akkodis ($7.0M), Peoplebank ($5.6M), Hoban ($4.9M), and DFP ($3.9M) collectively account for $85.9M — 33% of all professional services spend. This reflects agencies' heavy structural reliance on contingent workforce, particularly SA Police and Education.

Architecture accounts for $44.9M across Baukultur ($17.4M), Swanbury Penglase ($8.5M), Grieve Gillett ($7.7M), DesignInc Adelaide ($5.8M), and Russell & Yelland ($3.6M). This spend is almost entirely attributable to DIT's major hospital and infrastructure program — all five firms are engaged directly on DIT capital projects. Baukultur's position is notable: a single $17.3M contract for the SA Forensic Centre (lead project architect) makes it the third-largest professional services firm in the entire dataset.

Ernst & Young is the most widely deployed advisory firm in SA government, present across 9 agencies and 31 contracts. No other firm approaches this breadth. Its largest mandate — the SA Pathology Safety and Efficiency Review ($3M+) — is the most significant named advisory engagement in the dataset. KPMG leads by contract count (35 contracts, 7 agencies), reflecting a broad-based audit, assurance, and advisory practice rather than major single mandates.

Scyne Advisory ($3.4M, 17 contracts, 3 agencies) is building a consistent SA government position post-PwC rebrand. Its SA footprint spans Health (internal audit, operating model reform), Education (EMS assurance, risk, program review), and DIT (alliance audit). Average contract value of $202k reflects a high-volume, lower-margin audit and assurance model rather than large advisory mandates. This is a different positioning to EY's SA Health engagement, which involves substantive reform advice at higher individual contract values.

Engineering consulting ($22.2M across Aurecon, WSP, Mott MacDonald, Tonkin, Russell & Yelland) is the third-largest advisory category. All five firms are primarily engaged on DIT capital projects. Aurecon is active across 5 agencies (10 contracts), making it the most broadly deployed engineering advisory firm. MBB Group ($6.1M, 2 contracts) holds what appears to be construction management advisory on DIT projects — large individual engagements with a single client.

KPMG's 7-agency reach is similarly dispersed and count-heavy rather than value-heavy. This fragmentation suggests the SA government advisory market rewards persistent relationship-building and panel positioning rather than large single-agency mandates — with the partial exception of EY's SA Health and KPMG's DIT audit engagements.

Scyne Advisory's 3-agency, 17-contract position (Health, Education, DIT) is the most striking post-rebrand recovery in the dataset. Starting from a zero base in SA in October 2023, it has established audit and assurance mandates across three agencies in under two years — consistent with a deliberate strategy to reconstitute the former PwC Government Advisory client base under the new brand.
Data Limitations
1. Disclosure threshold and coverage gaps
SA CKAN disclosures are made by agencies on a voluntary or partially mandated basis. Not all agencies disclose all contracts, and the threshold for disclosure varies. Small advisory engagements below departmental reporting thresholds may not appear. The dataset likely understates the volume and value of small-value advisory work across all agencies, particularly for boutique SA-based consulting firms engaged on sub-threshold projects.
2. Undated records limit FY analysis
1,055 of 2,370 contract records (44%) carry no start date. These are predominantly from SA Police, SA Health, DPC, DSD, Defence SA, and SAFC, which publish aggregate staffing lists rather than individual contract awards. FY2024 and FY2025 figures in this report cover dated DIT and Education records only and do not represent the full agency-level spend for those years.
3. No subcontractor visibility
The CKAN dataset records only the prime contractor. In the major DIT hospital and transport projects, managing contractors engage hundreds of trade and specialist subcontractors — none visible here. The market position of firms operating as project subcontractors (particularly specialist trade contractors and advisory firms embedded inside managing contractor teams) is invisible in this dataset.
4. Aggregate rows and name inconsistency
Several agencies (notably SA Health and SAPOL) publish aggregate "total contractors" rows rather than individual disclosures. These have been excluded from contractor-level analysis but remain in agency-level totals where consistent with agency-published figures. Contractor names are inconsistently formatted across agencies and years — normalisation has been applied for known variants, but residual fragmentation across name variants for smaller firms is likely.
5. This dataset covers SA Government agencies only
Commonwealth agencies operating in South Australia (Defence, Home Affairs, Health) procure through AusTender, not CKAN. The two datasets do not overlap. For a full picture of contractor activity in SA — particularly for defence primes and infrastructure firms active on both Commonwealth and state programs — analysis must span both registers separately.
Methodology

Source. South Australian Government CKAN Open Data Portal. Contract disclosure data extracted for all available agencies. Records were loaded into a local SQLite database as part of the Orbit data pipeline.

Period. Records with start dates fall predominantly in FY2024 (July 2023 – June 2024) and FY2025 (July 2024 – June 2025). A substantial volume of records (1,055) carry no start date — these are included in agency totals and contractor rankings but excluded from year-attributed analysis. The CKAN portal does not consistently indicate the year of disclosure for undated records; notes fields suggest some date back to FY2022.

Exclusions. Aggregate placeholder rows — including "Total Contractors", "Contractors – Agency Staffing – Grand Total", and "Total Independent Contractors" — were excluded from all contractor-level analysis. Agency-level totals use the published figures inclusive of these rows where consistent with original source.

Name normalisation. Contractor names were normalised across case, punctuation, and legal suffix variants (Pty Ltd / Pty. Ltd. / Pty Limited). Known variants of the same entity were merged under a canonical name. Residual fragmentation across uncommon variants is possible for less-frequently appearing firms.

Consulting classification. Professional services firms are identified by company name against a curated list of known consulting, advisory, architecture, engineering, IT services, and recruitment firms. Description-keyword matching was not used; identification is based on firm identity only. This approach captures known firms reliably but will miss boutique or newly established advisory firms not on the reference list.
